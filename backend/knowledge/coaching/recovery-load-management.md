## Read recovery as a pattern

Recovery is the balance between training and competition load, life stress, and restoration. Interpret HRV, resting heart rate, sleep, readiness, subjective fatigue, and recent training together. A single unusual measure is not a diagnosis. Repeated suppressed recovery signals plus rising load justify reducing intensity or volume and reassessing.

Source: [Kellmann et al., Recovery and Performance in Sport: Consensus Statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29345524/).

## Return-to-training guardrail

When load has risen quickly, or wellbeing and recovery have deteriorated, prioritize a conservative adjustment: reduce intensity first, maintain only easy movement if it is tolerated, and reassess the next objective signals. Do not diagnose injury, illness, overtraining syndrome, or RED-S from watch data. Escalate persistent symptoms, chest pain, fainting, severe shortness of breath, or suspected injury to qualified medical care.

Source: [Soligard et al., IOC consensus on load and injury risk](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/27535989/).

## Recovery actions

Sleep opportunity, adequate energy intake, carbohydrate availability for demanding work, protein across the day, hydration, and reduced competing stressors are first-line recovery supports. Recommendations must match the athlete's goal, diet, medical context, and training demand; they are not a substitute for individualized clinical nutrition advice.

Source: [Kellmann et al., Recovery and Performance in Sport: Consensus Statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29345524/).
