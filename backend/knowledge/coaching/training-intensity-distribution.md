## Distribution models: pyramidal, polarized, and threshold-heavy

In endurance programming, training-intensity distribution (TID) describes how weekly volume is partitioned across intensity domains:
- **Pyramidal Model**: Low intensity forms the vast base, threshold volume is moderate, and severe high-intensity work is smallest (e.g. 75% low, 15–20% threshold, 5–10% high). Highly common in distance runners and rowers.
- **Polarized Model**: High low-intensity volume combined with severe high-intensity sessions, deliberately minimizing middle-zone threshold volume (e.g. 80% low, 5% threshold, 15% high).
- **Threshold-Heavy Model**: Compresses easy volume in favor of substantial tempo/threshold work. Frequently causes stagnation or overreaching in high-volume programs due to chronic autonomic fatigue.

Elite endurance athletes rarely adhere to a single model year-round; patterns naturally shift from pyramidal in base phases toward polarized or event-specific distributions as racing nears.

Source: [Stöggl & Sperlich, Training-Intensity Distribution in Elite Endurance Athletes review](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37964776/).

## The 80/20 observation versus rigid dogma

The popular "80/20 rule" originates from observational research by Stephen Seiler showing that world-class endurance athletes consistently execute ~80% of sessions at low aerobic intensity and ~20% as high-quality sessions.

However, 80/20 is a descriptive observation, not a dogmatic law:
- Quantifying by **session count** (e.g. 4 easy runs, 1 workout = 80/20) yields different ratios than **time in zone** (e.g. 92% low, 8% high) or **heart rate distribution**.
- A training block of 75/25 or 85/15 is not fundamentally flawed; training density must flex with athlete recovery, total volume, and training background.

Source: [Seiler, What is Best Practice for Training Intensity and Duration Distribution in Endurance Athletes?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20861519/).

## Evidence on distribution superiority and periodization

A comprehensive 2026 network meta-analysis evaluating endurance training distributions found no single model (polarized vs. pyramidal) to hold a definitive, statistically significant advantage across VO2max or time-trial performance, due to substantial confidence interval overlap across populations.

Coaching takeaway: Do not hunt for an absolute formula. Adhere to the core evidence-based principle: keep the vast majority of training volume genuinely easy to allow high repeatability, apply targeted quality work to address the athlete's specific limiter, and periodize intensity as the race approaches.

Source: [Network meta-analysis on endurance training intensity distributions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42171506/).
