## Daniels-style pace prescription

Daniels-style training separates easy running from marathon, threshold, interval, and repetition work. Use it as an intensity vocabulary, not as permission to force a pace when recovery signals are poor. For this athlete, anchor any suggested pace to current COROS threshold pace, threshold heart rate, fitness history, and recent execution. Do not reproduce proprietary VDOT tables; if precise race-derived paces are unavailable, prescribe an effort range and ask for a recent race or controlled test.

Source: Jack Daniels, *Daniels' Running Formula*, 4th ed. Human Kinetics, 2021.

## Friel-style zones and periodization

Friel-style planning organizes training around functional threshold and progressive blocks of base, build, peak, and recovery. Zone labels are only useful when their threshold anchor is current and sport-specific. Avoid stacking high-intensity sessions when readiness, HRV, sleep, or recent load indicates under-recovery. Treat a planned session as an intention that can be adjusted after checking athlete evidence.

Source: Joe Friel, *The Triathlete's Training Bible*, 5th ed. VeloPress, 2016.

## Progression guardrail

Use trend evidence rather than one workout to progress volume or intensity. Compare external load, internal response, recovery, wellbeing, and competition demands together. A sudden load increase can raise injury and illness risk; individual response varies, so coaching advice must state uncertainty when the data is incomplete.

Source: [Bourdon et al., Monitoring Athlete Training Loads: Consensus Statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/).
