## General, support, and specific preparation phases

Training periodization should systematically progress from building general physiological capacities to expressing race-specific fitness:
- **General Preparation Phase**: Establishes underlying capacity. For runners: high low-intensity aerobic volume, foundational compound lifting, strides, and basic threshold. For HYROX: running volume and maximal multi-joint force development.
- **Support Preparation Phase**: Bridges capacity toward race demands. For runners: extended threshold intervals and progressive long runs. For HYROX: station skill efficiency, sled technique, and short compromised intervals.
- **Specific Preparation Phase**: Integrates all race demands under fatigue. For runners: long runs with sustained marathon pace blocks and practiced fueling. For HYROX: race-weight compromise blocks and Roxzone transitions.

Source: [Norwegian world-class endurance coaching practices](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40278987/); Jack Daniels, *Daniels' Running Formula*, 4th ed.

## The cost and misuse of early full race simulations

While race simulations offer the highest specificity, they impose extreme autonomic and musculoskeletal fatigue costs:
- **Fatigue vs. Adaptation**: A 35 km marathon-pace run or a weekly full-intensity HYROX simulation causes severe glycogen depletion and tissue damage, requiring 7–14 days of suppressed training, which destroys consistency.
- **Principle of Phased Integration**: Build underlying physiological capacity first, then introduce race specificity in digestible blocks. Reserve complete simulations for 1–2 key rehearsals late in the build to calibrate pacing, fueling, and mental focus.

Source: [Bourdon et al., Monitoring Athlete Training Loads: Consensus Statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/); [Kellmann et al., Recovery and Performance in Sport](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29345524/).
