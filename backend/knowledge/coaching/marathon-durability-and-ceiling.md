## Physiological durability versus fresh ceiling

Endurance performance is not determined solely by laboratory tests conducted in a rested, fresh state:
- **Aerobic Ceiling**: What an athlete can produce when fully rested (VO2max, fresh LT2 pace, peak velocity).
- **Physiological Durability**: Defined as the time of onset and the magnitude of deterioration in physiological characteristics (such as running economy, heart rate drift, and threshold power) during prolonged exertion.

Athletes with identical fresh threshold paces frequently exhibit divergent marathon outcomes because their physiological degradation rates differ markedly over 42 kilometers.

Source: [Maunder et al., The Importance of 'Durability' in the Physiological Profiling of Endurance Athletes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33886100/); [Review on endurance durability as a distinct dimension](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40150840/).

## Workload decoupling onset and race performance

An epidemiological study of over 82,000 marathon runners demonstrated that internal-to-external workload decoupling (loss of pace efficiency relative to heart rate) began at an average distance of 25.2 ± 9.9 km:
- **Low Decoupling Group**: Onset delayed to ~33.4 km; athletes maintained a higher percentage of Critical Speed and achieved faster finish times.
- **High Decoupling Group**: Onset occurred early (~19.1 km), resulting in catastrophic late-race pace collapse.

A successful marathon is governed not merely by fresh speed, but by delaying physiological decoupling deep into the final 10 kilometers.

Source: [Smyth et al., Decoupling and marathon performance in 82,000 runners](https://link.springer.com/article/10.1007/s40279-022-01680-5).

## Multidimensional marathon determinants: LT1, LT2, and volume

In 97 recreational marathon runners, race speed demonstrated strong physiological correlations:
- First Lactate Threshold (LT1 / Aerobic Threshold): r = 0.791
- Second Lactate Threshold (LT2 / Anaerobic Threshold): r = 0.721
- Mean distance per training session: r = 0.563

Marathon pace is predominantly sustained near LT1 and moderate aerobic intensity. Expanding the lower threshold and building durable single-session volume is as critical as high-end threshold workouts.

Source: [Study on physiological predictors of marathon running performance](https://pmc.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/articles/PMC5703178/).

## Diagnosing and training marathon ceiling versus durability

- **If Ceiling is Limiting**: If target marathon pace is faster than or too close to current LT2 (e.g. target 4:59/km with LT2 at 5:10/km), the pace is physiologically inaccessible. Priorities: raise LT2, increase VO2max, and build speed reserve before marathon-specific volume.
- **If Durability is Limiting**: If target pace is well below fresh LT2 (e.g. target 4:59/km with LT2 at 4:35/km), yet marathon pace collapses after 28 km. Priorities: high weekly mileage, progressive long runs with marathon-effort blocks, intra-race carbohydrate fueling (60–90 g/h), and muscular fatigue resistance.

Rule: Ceiling determines what pace is possible. Durability determines how long it remains possible.

Source: [Maunder et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33886100/); Jack Daniels, *Daniels' Running Formula*, 4th ed.
