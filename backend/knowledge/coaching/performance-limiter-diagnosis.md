## Capacity, durability, and specificity framework

Evaluate athlete performance across three distinct dimensions rather than viewing fitness as a single number:
1. **Capacity**: What can the athlete produce when fresh? Defined by maximal oxygen uptake (VO2max), second lactate threshold (LT2), speed reserve, or maximal force.
2. **Durability**: How much capacity remains after prolonged work and cumulative fatigue? Reflects fatigue resistance and minimal physiological deterioration over time.
3. **Specificity**: Can the athlete express capacity under exact race conditions? Encompasses terrain, heat, pacing discipline, station transitions, and race-day nutrition.

Core principle: Goal pace defines what the race requires; it does not automatically indicate what training stimulus the athlete currently needs.

Source: Jack Daniels, *Daniels' Running Formula*, 4th ed.; [Maunder et al., The Importance of 'Durability' in the Physiological Profiling of Endurance Athletes](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33886100/).

## Diagnosing ceiling versus sustain limiters

Athletes with identical current performance and identical race targets frequently possess opposing performance phenotypes:
- **Ceiling Limiter (Capacity Problem)**: The athlete exhibits high durability, low pace decay, and strong long runs, but lacks top-end speed or aerobic ceiling. For a sub-40 minute 10K (4:00/km), an athlete whose LT2 is 4:30/km cannot sustain goal pace because their ceiling is too low. Priorities: raise LT2, increase VO2max, improve velocity at VO2max, and build speed reserve.
- **Sustain Limiter (Durability Problem)**: The athlete possesses fast 400m/1km times, a high VO2max, and a strong threshold, but suffers late-race pace collapse. Giving this athlete more high-intensity speed work is counterproductive. Priorities: expand easy aerobic volume, improve threshold durability, practice pacing, and build fatigue resistance.

Source: [Casado et al., Training-Intensity Distribution in Middle- and Long-Distance Runners](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34749417/); [Maunder et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33886100/).

## Goal pace versus physiological demand

Prescribing goal-pace intervals (e.g. 10 x 1 km at target marathon or 10K pace) without diagnosing the underlying limiter often fails. If the pace sits above the athlete's current threshold, completing it with generous recovery creates artificial interval success without establishing an executable race effort.

Diagnose the limiter first across sports (running, HYROX, cycling, triathlon):
- If capacity is limiting: expand the aerobic ceiling, threshold power/pace, or maximal strength.
- If durability is limiting: accumulate low-intensity volume, long-duration steady work, and fueling tolerance.
- If specificity is limiting: introduce compromised running, race simulation blocks, and environment-specific practice.

Source: [Bourdon et al., Monitoring Athlete Training Loads: Consensus Statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/); Jack Daniels, *Daniels' Running Formula*, 4th ed.
