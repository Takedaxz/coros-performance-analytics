## Strides for neuromuscular coordination and stiffness

Strides maintain neuromuscular speed sensations without creating metabolic fatigue during high-volume training:
- **Format**: 4–6 repetitions of 15–30 seconds, accelerating smoothly to ~90–95% maximum sprint effort with relaxed shoulders and quick turnover.
- **Recovery**: 60–120 seconds of easy walking or gentle jogging until respiration and heart rate settle completely.
- **Purpose**: Reinforces leg stiffness, motor unit recruitment, pelvic alignment, and cadence. Strides do not accumulate blood lactate or stress VO2max.

Rule: A stride session should leave the athlete feeling physically sharper and primed, never fatigued.

Source: Jack Daniels, *Daniels' Running Formula*, 4th ed.; [Bourdon et al., 2017](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/).

## Resistance and plyometric training for running economy

Running performance is not determined by cardiovascular capacity alone. Systematic reviews demonstrate that heavy resistance training (e.g. squats, deadlifts, lunges) and plyometrics (e.g. pogo jumps, bounding) substantially enhance running economy in endurance athletes without inducing unwanted hypertrophy or altering VO2max.

Mechanisms include increased lower-limb tendon stiffness, improved rate of force development, enhanced elastic energy return, and reduced relative muscle activation per foot strike.

Source: [Systematic review and meta-analysis on strength training and running economy](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/38165636/).

## Biomechanical and metabolic components of running output

Running velocity is governed by the integrated triad of performance:
Oxygen Delivery (Cardiac Output & Capillaries) + Metabolic Capacity (Mitochondrial ATP & LT2) + Biomechanical Efficiency (Running Economy & Stiffness) = Running Output.

An athlete with a moderate VO2max can outperform an athlete with a superior VO2max if superior running economy and tendon elasticity reduce the oxygen cost of their target pace.

Source: [Bourdon et al., Monitoring Athlete Training Loads: Consensus Statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/).
