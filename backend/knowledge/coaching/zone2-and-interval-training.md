## Zone 2 builds repeatable aerobic volume

Zone 2 is low-intensity continuous work that supports aerobic capacity, mitochondrial and capillary adaptations, fat oxidation, and the ability to accumulate training volume with relatively low fatigue. Its primary value is not a single exceptional workout; it is making it possible to train consistently, recover, and complete future key sessions well.

Source: [Bourdon et al., training-load monitoring consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/); User-contributed coaching framework.

## Three-domain intensity model and threshold boundaries

In exercise physiology, exercise intensity is divided into three distinct metabolic domains anchored by two physiological thresholds:
1. **Low / Moderate Domain (Below LT1 / First Lactate Threshold)**: Blood lactate remains at baseline (~1.5–2.0 mmol/L). Fats provide substantial fuel; ventilation is comfortable.
2. **Heavy Domain (Between LT1 and LT2 / Second Lactate Threshold)**: Blood lactate rises but reaches a steady state. Glycogen consumption accelerates.
3. **Severe Domain (Above LT2 / Critical Speed / CP)**: Lactate and oxygen uptake drift toward maximum; fatigue occurs within minutes.

Caution: Watch platform zones differ. A 5-zone watch system (such as COROS or Garmin) does not align 1:1 with the 3-domain physiological model. Watch "Zone 2" often encompasses upper moderate work unless calibrated to individual threshold test results.

Source: Jack Daniels, *Daniels' Running Formula*, 4th ed.; [Maunder et al., 2021](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/33886100/).

## Detecting low aerobic intensity and common mistakes

Do not rely on crude population formulas like 220 - age or MAF 180 (180 - age). Two athletes of the same age can differ by 25 bpm in maximum heart rate and exhibit completely different LT1 thresholds. Use calibrated, multi-modal verification:
- **Talk Test**: Able to speak continuous, full conversational sentences without gasping.
- **RPE (Perceived Exertion)**: Sits around 2–4 out of 10.
- **Heart Rate**: Calibrated against a lab-tested LT1, field lactate test, or established Lactate Threshold Heart Rate (LTHR).
- **Blood Lactate**: Ideally below ~2.0 mmol/L, keeping in mind that ambient heat, glycogen depletion, and test protocols influence readings.

Source: [Casado et al., Training-Intensity Distribution in Middle- and Long-Distance Runners](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/34749417/).

## Cardiovascular drift and signal interpretation

During long sessions, heart rate often drifts upward even when pace or power is held constant (e.g. 5:50/km at 140 bpm for the first 30 minutes drifting to 150 bpm by minute 90). This cardiovascular drift is driven by rising core temperature, dehydration, reduced stroke volume, and neuromuscular fatigue—not an immediate loss of fitness.

Rule: Heart rate is an informative physiological feedback signal, not an absolute speed limit. Do not force extreme pace drops solely because of minor late-session drift if effort remains conversational.

Source: [Bourdon et al., Monitoring Athlete Training Loads: Consensus Statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/).

## Intervals provide efficient but costly stimulus

Intervals alternate hard work with recovery. They can efficiently develop VO2max, cardiac output, running economy, lactate tolerance, and high-speed performance when training time is limited. Their recovery cost is higher, so more intervals are not automatically better. Too much intensity can reduce key-session quality, turn easy days into moderate days, increase injury risk, and reduce consistency.

Source: [Kellmann et al., Recovery and Performance in Sport consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29345524/).

## Choose the balance by need and recovery

Zone 2 and interval work produce overlapping aerobic adaptations. The practical difference is the size of the stimulus, fatigue cost, and repeatable volume. Build easy volume when aerobic durability is limited; add intensity when the athlete has enough base and is recovering well; reduce intensity when recovery is deteriorating. When time is constrained, interval work can offer more stimulus per hour, but it does not replace an aerobic base.

Source: [Seiler, What is Best Practice for Training Intensity and Duration Distribution?](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/20861519/).
