## Zone 2 builds repeatable aerobic volume

Zone 2 is low-intensity continuous work that supports aerobic capacity, mitochondrial and capillary adaptations, fat oxidation, and the ability to accumulate training volume with relatively low fatigue. Its primary value is not a single exceptional workout; it is making it possible to train consistently, recover, and complete future key sessions well.

## Intervals provide efficient but costly stimulus

Intervals alternate hard work with recovery. They can efficiently develop VO2max, cardiac output, running economy, lactate tolerance, and high-speed performance when training time is limited. Their recovery cost is higher, so more intervals are not automatically better. Too much intensity can reduce key-session quality, turn easy days into moderate days, increase injury risk, and reduce consistency.

## Choose the balance by need and recovery

Zone 2 and interval work both produce overlapping aerobic adaptations. The practical difference is the size of the stimulus, fatigue cost, and repeatable volume. Build easy volume when aerobic durability is limited; add intensity when the athlete has enough base and is recovering well; reduce intensity when recovery is deteriorating. When time is constrained, interval work can offer more stimulus per hour, but it does not replace an aerobic base.

## Periodize the proportion rather than banning a method

A base phase emphasizes easy volume, long work, and aerobic development, but can still retain small amounts of strides, hill sprints, threshold, or intervals. As race day approaches, training generally moves from general aerobic work toward threshold, VO2max, race pace, and event-specific execution. The coaching question is not “Zone 2 or intervals?” but “Which stimulus is needed now, and can this athlete recover from it?”
