## Aerobic base and long rides

Build cycling durability through regular low-intensity riding, gradually longer rides, and recovery sufficient to absorb the workload. Use heart rate, power when available, perceived effort, sleep, and recent load together; no single metric establishes readiness. Event preparation should include terrain, position, cadence, and nutrition practice specific to the target ride.

Source: [Bourdon et al., training-load monitoring consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/).

## Threshold and interval work

Use threshold or interval sessions to target a specific limitation, then protect recovery around them. Avoid adding intervals, long rides, strength work, and life stress simultaneously. If power and heart rate disagree unusually, describe the discrepancy and check fatigue, heat, fueling, equipment, and data quality before prescribing more intensity.

Source: [Kellmann et al., Recovery and Performance in Sport consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/29345524/).

## Cycling race execution

Practice the fueling, hydration, clothing, pacing, and equipment plan in training rather than introducing it on race day. For a target event, build a plan around duration, environment, aid access, and individual tolerance; general nutrition guidance does not replace sport-dietitian advice for medical, gastrointestinal, or weight-management issues.

Source: [Nutrition and Athletic Performance position statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11145214/).
