## 5K and 10K development

For 5K and 10K preparation, build consistent easy aerobic running first, then add one event-relevant quality stimulus at a time: threshold-oriented work, faster interval work, hills, or race-specific pacing. Keep easy days genuinely easy so quality sessions are executable. Use current threshold and recent workout response to select intensity; do not infer a race pace from an old personal best alone.

Source: Jack Daniels, *Daniels' Running Formula*, 4th ed. Human Kinetics, 2021; [Bourdon et al., training-load monitoring consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/).

## Half marathon and marathon development

For half marathon and marathon preparation, prioritize durable weekly running, a progressive long run, event-specific aerobic work, and practiced fueling. Add intensity only when recent recovery supports it. A long run is not a race simulation every week; preserve enough recovery to complete the next key session well. Adjust volume and long-run progression for current history, terrain, injury history, and available training days.

Source: [Nutrition and Athletic Performance position statement](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/11145214/); [Bourdon et al., training-load monitoring consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/).

## 50K, 100K, and ultra preparation

Ultra preparation adds time-on-feet, terrain-specific climbing and descending, pacing discipline, equipment practice, and a tested fueling plan. Progress the longest outings and back-to-back stress conservatively; prioritize completing planned training healthy over maximizing any single session. Practice race nutrition in training, because gastrointestinal tolerance and logistics are individual.

Source: [ISSN position stand: single-stage ultra-marathon training and racing](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/31699159/).

## Taper and race week

For endurance events, a taper usually reduces volume while retaining some frequency and event-relevant intensity. Meta-analysis evidence supports individualized tapers of up to 21 days with a progressive volume reduction; do not apply a fixed percentage without considering fatigue, event length, and training history. Race-week advice must account for sleep, travel, weather, and current recovery.

Source: [Wang et al., endurance taper systematic review and meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37163550/).
