## Controlled threshold volume and lactate regulation

The Norwegian method is defined by accumulating substantial threshold volume while strictly constraining internal physiological stress:
- **Lactate Regulation**: Athletes monitor capillary blood lactate during intervals, intentionally capping levels between 2.0 and 3.5 mmol/L (safely below LT2 / anaerobic threshold).
- **Disciplined Execution**: Workouts are never raced or pushed to exhaustion; preserving a controlled sub-threshold state minimizes autonomic nervous system disturbance and enables rapid day-to-day recovery.

Source: [Narrative review on the Norwegian training model](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/42389749/).

## Interval structuring versus continuous threshold blocks

Accumulating threshold volume via intervals (e.g. 6 x 10 minutes or 5 x 6 minutes with short ~60-second recoveries) is physiologically superior to continuous long tempos (e.g. 60 minutes uninterrupted):
- **Drift Attenuation**: Controlled intervals interrupt progressive cardiovascular and metabolic drift, keeping blood lactate stable.
- **Biomechanical Preservation**: Short rest intervals allow runners to maintain optimal cadence and posture throughout high total volume.

Controlled trials comparing continuous moderate work with split interval structures confirm that continuous sessions elicit significantly higher cardiac drift, lactate accumulation, and perceived exertion for identical mechanical work.

Source: [Study on cardiovascular drift in continuous versus split endurance sessions](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/39139482/).

## Double threshold application and recreational athlete caveats

The "double threshold" day (e.g. AM 5 x 6 minutes at 2.5 mmol/L and PM 10 x 1 km at 3.0 mmol/L) is widely publicized, but requires critical context:
- Neither session is run to failure; both are sub-maximal, tightly managed endurance blocks.
- World-class Norwegian coaches report that double threshold represents only ~15–20% of training, supported by 150–200+ km of weekly low-intensity running and years of physiological adaptation.

Recreational warning: Without extensive aerobic durability, professional recovery, and lactate verification, copying double threshold days leads to rapid overreaching and injury.

Rule: The Norwegian advantage is physiological control and repeatability, not suffering twice in one day.

Source: [Norwegian world-class endurance coaching practices](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/40278987/).
