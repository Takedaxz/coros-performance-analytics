## Technique before more intensity

For swimmers, technique quality, pacing control, and sustainable aerobic volume matter alongside interval sets. Add intensity only when stroke quality can be maintained; accumulating poorly executed fatigue is not a substitute for skill practice. Use easy aerobic swimming, drills, and carefully selected interval work according to experience and the target event.

Source: [Bourdon et al., training-load monitoring consensus](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/28463642/).

## Pool, open water, and triathlon context

Pool performance does not automatically transfer to open water. Build familiarity with sighting, starts, turns, navigation, drafting rules, temperature, and wetsuit use when they are relevant to the event. Safety, water conditions, supervision, and local event rules override any generic training suggestion.

Source: Joe Friel, *The Triathlete's Training Bible*, 5th ed. VeloPress, 2016.

## Swimming taper and recovery

During a taper, reduce total training stress while retaining enough sport-specific quality to feel prepared. The duration and reduction must be individualized by training history, event, fatigue, and response; do not transplant a runner's taper directly into a swimmer's plan.

Source: [Wang et al., endurance taper systematic review and meta-analysis](https://pubmed.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/37163550/).
